# Car shapes: Hypercar reference rows

Ticket 59 (pit-wall thread 124 #8116-#8125). One row per Hypercar model in Botkin's results files since 2026-09-01, with the public numbers and the one cue that makes the model read. Research only: no geometry is built from this until steward and scout have checked it. Nothing image-based goes in the repo; every cue is words with a link.

How to read a row:
- **Dimensions** are metres-as-published, each with the source it came from. A figure that is a *regulation cap* (the class limit, not the car) says so. A figure no source gives says "not found"; nothing is guessed.
- **Cue** is the silhouette feature that should survive 375 pt from Chase and High. *Sourced* means a page says it in words (linked). *Visual* means the sources I could read in text do not describe it, so a seat has to confirm it from a photo of the car (the linked article carries one) before it is built.
- **Sets** names the shape parameter the cue moves, as a direction from the Hyper class hull in `carShapes.ts` (5.0 x 2.0 m, wheels 1.55 / -1.50 m, so a 3.05 m wheelbase). The names follow scout's #8116 list; steward checks each exists in wrench's parameter table.

## What the numbers say

- Six of the ten share one set of limits. The LMDh rules fix the wheelbase at 3,148 mm (+/-5) and cap the car at 5,100 x 2,000 x 1,060 mm ([Motor1 on the BMW](https://www.motor1.com/news/590214/bmw-m-hybrid-v8-lmdh-prototype/), [Porsche newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/es_ES/competicion/2023/es-porsche-963-hypercar-gtp-prototipo-tecnologia-31044.html)); BMW, Cadillac, Porsche, Alpine, Lamborghini and Genesis (Oreca chassis) are LMDh. The published lengths and widths of those six differ by under 2 %, as #6866 measured, so **length and width cannot tell them apart**. The class hull's 3.05 m wheelbase is 0.10 m short of what they run.
- The numbers that do differ are **height** (1,055 to 1,180 mm, about 12 %) and, for three LMH cars, **length/width/wheelbase**: Valkyrie 4.5 m long, Peugeot 2.08 m wide on a 3.045 m wheelbase, Toyota 4.9 m long. Those are the dimension-backed differences; the rest is cue.
- All ten are mid-engine prototypes, so layout does not separate them the way it does the GT3s; it is left out of the table.
- Where a Wikipedia infobox and a team or maker page disagree, both are listed. I did not pick one.

## Gaps and questions for the checkers

1. **Cues with no text source** (visual): Toyota's tall front fenders, Ferrari's split nose, Porsche's fin, Alpine's silhouette, Genesis's side line, the Valkyrie's tunnels as a side feature. The pages I could read do not describe these. They need someone to look at a photo of each car (every row links an article with one) and write the cue in words before any geometry.
2. **Peugeot has two shapes in the sim**: the wingless launch car and the 2024 winged one (Le Mans Ultimate lists a separate "9X8 2024", [site](https://lemansultimate.com/cars/peugeot-9x8-2024)). One field string or two in his results files? If both, the 9X8 is a wing option on one shape, not two shapes.
3. **Height** is the dimension that moves (1,055 to 1,180 mm), but the LMDh cap is 1,060, so Lamborghini's 1,170 (Wikipedia) and BMW's reported 1,200 contradict the cap. I did not resolve this; a team page or the game's own value would.
4. **The wheelbase cap (3,148 mm) is 0.10 m longer than the class hull's 3.05 m.** Wheels placed from each model's wheelbase, as steward asked (#8117), will move on every LMDh car even before cues.
5. Not covered: Isotta Fraschini and Glickenhaus/Vanwall (keep the class hull, #8116).
