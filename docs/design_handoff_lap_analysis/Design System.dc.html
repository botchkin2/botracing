Design system · v0.1 · React Native reference

Lap analysis

Built from the four locked views: Sessions, Session, Compare and Corner (Directions, turn 4). The system is dense and dark first. Each color has one meaning, numbers always carry units, and every chart has a one-line explanation. Token names are written so they map directly to a React Native theme object.

01

Color rules

Amber is UI only
Selection, highlight, primary buttons, the chart cursor, pit markers. It is never used as a lap color.
Purple means best
Session best lap and best sectors, and nothing else.
−+
Faster / slower
Always paired with a − or + sign. Faster is bright and slower is dark, so the pair still reads without color vision.
Lap colors are only for laps
The reference lap is white, and selected laps take the next colors in a fixed order. From 7 to 19 laps, each lap keeps a faded version of its color; from 20, laps turn grey. The reference and the lap you tap always stay full strength.

A chart holds 1–3 channels. Line style shows the channel (solid, dashed, dotted) and color shows the lap, so the two never compete. Same-kind channels share a scale, and mixed units keep their own scales. Overlay pills toggle channels on the focused chart. Chart rows in the editor have channel chips, "+ overlay", and reorder and delete controls.
Many laps · 7–19 tinted · 20+ grey
12 laps · tinted
20 laps · grey
With 7–19 laps, each lap keeps a hue from the lap order but drops to low chroma (lap.tint, C 0.07), 1.2 pt and 55% opacity. You can still tell laps apart where they split, and the overlay doesn't turn into noise. At 20 or more, laps go to muted grey at 1 pt and 45%. In both modes the reference (white) and the tapped lap (full-strength lap.1) are drawn at 2.3 pt on top, and only those two show values in the chart header.
Lap-time bars · whole session
Bars show each lap against the median: up is faster, clamped at ±1.5 s. The best lap is purple, selected laps take their lap color, and the highlighted lap gets an amber frame. Excluded laps are outlined stubs on the baseline. Stints are divided by thin rules, and pit stops are dashed amber lines.
